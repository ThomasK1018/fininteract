Large language model (LLM) agents are increasingly utilized in financial analysis to answer questions in regulatory filings~\citep{islam2023financebench,chen2021finqa,reddy2024docfinqa}, and carry out multi-step research and retrieval over corporate disclosures~\citep{bigeard2025financeagent,choi2025finagentbench,krumdick2024bizbench,xie2024finben}. The agent reads a user's question, searches through relevant filings, and returns the answer for downstream actions.

However, financial questions are often deceptively under-specified. Financial terminology is highly specific, and a natural language question may map to several defensible readings for different numbers, e.g., in the question \emph{``What was Meta Platforms' operating income?''} (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}), an agent must decide whether the user means the consolidated GAAP figure (\$46.75B for FY2023) or the Family of Apps segment (\$62.87B), both being programmatically verifiable against SEC facts. A capable agent should recognize the ambiguity and follow up with the user instead of directly committing to a plausible but unintended reading.

Financial QA benchmarks typically evaluate answers against a specified interpretation~\citep{islam2023financebench,chen2021finqa,reddy2024docfinqa,krumdick2024bizbench}. Broader suites such as FinBen cover additional tasks~\citep{xie2024finben}, but do not provide our controlled paired-reading clarification protocol. A single reference answer cannot distinguish resolving an ambiguity from guessing the reference reading. We call this blind spot the \emph{single-gold illusion} (Table~\ref{tab:comparison} in Appendix~\ref{app:related}), extending questions studied in general ambiguous QA to financial filings~\citep{min2020ambigqa,li2025condambigqa,kuhn2022clam}. We study three research questions: \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, topsep=3pt, itemsep=1pt, parsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{RQ1}: Do frontier models recognize financial report ambiguity?
    \item \textbf{RQ2}: Which financial ambiguity categories lead to the most failures?
    \item \textbf{RQ3}: Can interventions at inference or training time improve resolution?
\end{itemize}

We design the benchmark to cover ambiguity in public filings, diagnose category-specific failures, support semi-automated construction without answer leakage through the disambiguating context, and permit expert validation (Sec.~\ref{sec:methodology}).

We release \fininteract,\footnote{\url{https://thomask1018.github.io/fininteract-project/}} a bilingual benchmark in English and Chinese containing 173 instances, classified into five financial ambiguity categories. Each question $Q$ is paired with a \emph{default} interpretation recording a possible non-expert assumption and an \emph{intended} interpretation fixed by the disambiguating context. By grading the same model outputs against the default and intended interpretations, we effectively expose this single-gold illusion. \fininteract includes a skill metric that localizes which clarification ability the model lacks, and evaluates the agents under a ReAct protocol where the user simulator answers only from the disambiguating context (Figure~\ref{fig:framework}) to measure the agent's quality of clarify-then-integrate action.

Our evaluation answers the research questions in turn. First, models often name a relevant category without asking a question that resolves the ambiguity; even after a resolving question, integration can fail (Sec.~\ref{sec:ceiling}). Second, targeting is uneven across categories, with a recognition-policy blind spot on an exploratory nine-instance sample (Sec.~\ref{sec:skills}). Third, category-guided supervision raises Qwen3-14B's interaction accuracy from 32.4\% to 60.1\%, chiefly on the Chinese split (Sec.~\ref{sec:trainsignal}).

This paper makes three contributions: \begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Problem definition.} We formalize ambiguity in financial questions with five categories in standard accounting and disclosure practice (Sec.~\ref{sec:task}).
  \item \textbf{Benchmark.} We introduce \fininteract, a bilingual financial ambiguity dataset of 173 instances with paired default and intended interpretations verified by domain experts (Sec.~\ref{sec:methodology}).
  \item \textbf{Empirical findings.} Our evaluation distinguishes category targeting, ambiguity resolution, and answer integration, and tests category-conditioned interventions (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp}).
\end{enumerate}
