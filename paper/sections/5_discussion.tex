In our study, we observe that \textbf{categories are different learning problems.} While some clarification abilities are solved by scale, others, notably recognition basis, resist it, so a single aggregate score hides where
the difficulty sits and curricula should budget data per category. On the other hand, \textbf{elicitation is a trainable skill current models lack.} Progress will come less from larger backbones, which barely move interaction accuracy, than from policies and objectives that reward asking on the right category at the right time. Nevertheless, even by including annotation for domain experts, it is still possible to retain \textbf{ambiguity in Human-AI collaboration} because models confidently return the default reading, motivating a safe behavior of asking instead of answering, hence the development of metrics that credit well-timed clarification and calibration process. While the current \fininteract is semi-automated, ideally a benchmark should be able to extend itself on categories and paired default/intended construction whose evidence is abundant, so as to \textbf{transfer to further categories, languages and other high-stakes domains} where ambiguity is commonly observed. 

We note the following limitations of this study: \textbf{1) Scale} where a 173 instances corpus is considerably small given the need of construction rigor, \textbf{2) Language balance} where current corpus split between English and Chinese is $53:120$, which could benefit models pre-trained in certain language, \textbf{3) Dominating categories} with entity scope ($n{=}94$) and metric definition ($n{=}63$), compared to the blind spot recognition policy with only $n{=}9$, which may suggest existing model is not well-trained enough to even generate such instance in the first place, \textbf{4) Generator and Evaluator bias} due to API budget constraints, as we hire GPT-5 family mainly for construction and simulation, which may bias towards the performance of GPT-5 family. \textbf{5) Data contamination} whereas instances are freshly constructed from FY2022-2025 facts where recently released models might have been pre-trained on. We include extended discussion in Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}.
