\subsection{Evaluation setup}
\textbf{Agent architecture.} We evaluate agents under the ReAct~\citep{yao2023react} framework in the seven configurations of Table~\ref{tab:modes}. The \textbf{interact} action presents a yes-or-no question to the simulated user, who must respond ``yes'', ``no'', or ``I don't know''.

\textbf{Prompting strategy.} \fininteract evaluates interactive agents under several interaction protocols: answer-only, answer with search, answer with search and interaction, and the category-conditioned policies. All models run under the same ReAct system prompt. Additional details are in Appendix~\ref{sec:error}.

\textbf{Models.} We evaluate a diverse set of models. The proprietary set comprises the GPT family (GPT-5.6, GPT-5, GPT-4o, and GPT-5-mini), Claude-Sonnet-5, Gemini-3.5-flash, Grok-4.5, DeepSeek-V4-flash, GLM-5.2, and Kimi-K3. The open-source set comprises the dense Qwen3 family (4B, 8B, 14B, and 32B) and two mixture-of-experts models, Qwen3-30B-A3B and Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. Proprietary models are accessed through a unified OpenRouter harness.

\subsection{Overall performance}
\label{sec:ceiling}
Frontier models remain vulnerable to financial ambiguity (Table~\ref{tab:main}). The best full-scale model reaches 28.9\% intended-answer accuracy, while the best cross-vendor pilot reaches 44.0\%; the latter uses only 50 instances and is not directly comparable. Adding interaction has a non-positive accuracy difference for eleven of sixteen models. High category-targeting AC@1 does not establish that the clarification resolves the intended reading.

We therefore re-score stored questions with a resolution-sufficiency judge: a truthful yes/no response must distinguish the paired readings. For GPT-5, resolving AC@1 is 0.27 versus touch AC@1 of 0.86; for GPT-4o, it is 0.02 versus 0.94 (Appendix~\ref{app:additional_validation}). These model-judged diagnostics have not received separate human validation. They distinguish inadequate clarification from failures to integrate an informative response.

Table~\ref{tab:ceiling} brackets performance with forced clarification and supplied intent. Four forced clarification rounds reduce accuracy for the three GPT models. Supplying $C$ together with both evidence spans raises their accuracy to 93--95\%, and the two listed Qwen models to 90--92\%. This oracle changes both intent availability and evidence presentation. A matched pilot reaches 92\% with both, versus 4\% with interpretation alone and 6\% with evidence alone (Appendix~\ref{app:additional_validation}). \textbf{Finding 1: resolving ambiguity requires useful clarification and answer integration; category targeting alone is insufficient.}

\subsection{Sensitivity to the gold interpretation}
\label{sec:illusion}

The paired answers let us re-grade identical outputs against two defensible readings (Table~\ref{tab:default}). GPT-4o's retrieval-assisted outputs match the default on 64 of 173 items (37.0\%) but the intended answer on 21 (12.1\%), approximately a threefold ratio. Across eight models with nonzero intended accuracy, the ratios range from 0.9 to 6.2, with a median of 1.8; seven exceed one.

This effect is not uniform across language and filing regime. All eight English-split ratios are below one (0.15--0.8), while seven of eight Chinese-split ratios exceed one (range 0.9--16.8). These are controlled re-gradings of \fininteract outputs, not measurements of inflation in existing benchmarks. \textbf{Finding 2: a single gold interpretation can substantially change measured accuracy, with direction and magnitude depending on the evaluated subset.}

\subsection{Targeting varies by ambiguity category}
\label{sec:skills}
Aggregate AC@1 hides where clarification actually succeeds, so we decompose targeting by ambiguity category (Table~\ref{tab:category_targeting}). Models name the correct category almost always for entity scope, metric definition, and temporal scope, but never for recognition policy, where AC@1 is 0 for every model evaluated. A case study makes the missed distinction concrete. Asked ``What were General Electric's revenues?'', a model may answer \$67.95B or \$26.79B depending on the recognition basis: the former is GAAP revenue combining over-time service recognition with point-in-time product recognition, the latter counts only revenue recognized at a point in time. Models return a single figure without asking which basis is meant, defaulting to the assumption that ``revenue'' is well defined and passing over the ASC~606 distinction between point-in-time and over-time recognition. We read this as evidence that a domain-specific reporting practice is not reflected in current models' general clarification behavior, which is what motivates the category-conditioned interventions of Sec.~\ref{sec:trainsignal}.

This last observation carries an important caveat. Recognition policy is one of our three exploratory categories, with only n = 9 instances, and an item-level audit (Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}) finds most of them to be ASC~606 timing disaggregations where neither value is the total a non-expert would assume. Our two human annotators also never targeted the category, so the blind spot is at least not model-specific. \textbf{Finding 3: targeting accuracy is uneven across ambiguity categories; entity, metric, and temporal ambiguity are reliably named, while recognition policy is never raised, on preliminary evidence from a nine-instance category.}

\subsection{Ambiguity category as training signal}
\label{sec:trainsignal}
Category-guided supervision improves end-to-end interaction on an open-model evaluation stack. We fine-tune Qwen3-14B on 231 demonstrations from 85 training IDs, none shared with the 173 benchmark IDs. With ordinary retrieval and locally served judges, interaction accuracy rises from 32.4\% to 60.1\%, while search-only accuracy changes from 34.1\% to 31.8\% (Table~\ref{tab:sft14b}). The paired gain is 27.7 percentage points (63 wins, 15 losses). It is concentrated in Chinese, consistent with the demonstration mix; English accuracy remains similar.

Earlier Qwen3-4B experiments increase category targeting from 25 to 69 AC@1 after supervised fine-tuning, but their answer accuracy is confounded by disambiguated retrieval and is not evidence for end-to-end improvement. We retain those behavioral analyses in Appendix~\ref{sec:grpo}. At inference time, Category-Aware ReAct increases GPT-5 accuracy from 34\% to 46\% on the first 50 release instances (3 EN, 47 ZH); this is a preliminary, language-skewed pilot. \textbf{Finding 4: category-guided supervision can improve intent resolution, but the gain depends on language and evaluation conditions.}
